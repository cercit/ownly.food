# Ownly Together — Chennai Group Food Ordering Survey

**Status:** Ready-to-adapt research instrument. This surveys **current group ordering**, not usage of Ownly Together in Chennai.  
**Goal:** Quantify patterns found through interviews: coordination work, cost sharing, alternatives, trust, barriers, and repeat use.  
**Audience:** Adults (18+) in Chennai, including group-order Hosts and Members.  
**Estimated length:** Approximately 7–9 minutes; about 20–23 prompts including screening and optional items.  
**Recommended form platforms:** Google Forms or Microsoft Forms; configure single-choice, multi-select, linear scale and branching as shown.

> **Important:** Ownly Together is a proposed Chennai launch concept. No survey responses have been gathered. Any examples marked **HYPOTHETICAL** are illustrations, not research findings.

## 1. Survey introduction and consent (required)

**Respondent-facing copy:**

> We are studying how adults in Chennai order food with other people. Your answers will inform a possible group-ordering experience. Participation is voluntary. You may skip optional questions or stop at any time. Please avoid entering names, phone numbers, UPI IDs or order references. We will report only aggregated, de-identified patterns. **Before distributing this survey, the team must insert accurate information about data storage, access, retention, withdrawal options and a contact for research questions.** This survey does not require payment or access to your personal accounts.

**S0. Do you consent to participate?** [Single choice; required]
- Yes, I agree → S1
- No → End survey

Do not collect recordings or identifying information through this form. If contact details are needed for research follow-up, request them in a separate opt-in form.

## 6. Optional unbranded concept check (after behaviour)

**Read to respondent:**

> Imagine a food-ordering option where one person creates an order, others join by link and add their own items, and each person pays their own share. A previous group can be reused for another order.

| ID | Question | Answer options and setup |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | How relevant, if at all, would this option be to occasions when you order food with other people? | 1 Not relevant / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 Very relevant / Not sure (linear scale) |
| C2 | What questions, concerns, or missing details would you have? | Optional short answer (max 300 characters) |

**Caution:** Relevance ratings describe *stated perceptions*, not download intent, actual adoption, willingness to pay, or feature-market fit. Do not introduce the brand before the behavioural questions.

## 7. Question-bias audit

| Risk | Risky phrasing | Neutral version used |
|---|---|---|
| Leading | “Was WhatsApp a hassle?” | “How did your group coordinate choices?” (Q3) |
| Assumptive | “What went wrong?” | “Which, if any, happened?” plus “None” (Q7) |
| Social desirability | “Would you definitely download it?” | Ask past unfamiliar-link behaviour (Q11) |
| Double-barrelled | “Was pricing and ordering confusing?” | Separate payment clarity (Q6) and effort (Q8) |
| Forced choice | “Which payment problem did you face?” | Allow “Not applicable,” “None,” or “Unsure” (Q5–Q7) |
| Hypothetical overclaim | “Would you use Ownly weekly?” | Optional relevance rating; label non-predictive (C1) |

**Pilot test with 3–5 Chennai adults**, including a Host and a Member, before distribution. Check comprehension in English/Tamil, exclusive options, the time to complete, and branching.

## 8. Coding and analysis plan

**Minimum segmentation:** Host / Member / Both; office / PG-flat / hostel / home; geographic area; recent group-order frequency. Do not report tiny subgroups as representative of a neighbourhood.

**Core calculations** (exclude blank/“Don't know” from relevant denominators and report `n`):
- **Pain incidence:** respondents selecting each Q7 friction ÷ eligible Q7 respondents.
- **Cost clarity:** Q6 mean and full 1–5 distribution, including `n`.
- **Effort:** Q8 distribution by Host vs Member.
- **Payment follow-up:** Hosts answering H2 Yes ÷ eligible Hosts answering H2.
- **Repeat behaviour:** frequency distribution of Q10.
- **Concept relevance:** C1 distribution, clearly labelled *stated*.

**Interpretation:** Cross-tabulate Q7 × role and Q10 × Q7; check whether themes from interviews recur. Code Q12/C2 into themes with exact anonymous quotes. Do not treat convenience-sample percentages as Chennai population estimates.

## 9. Illustrative analysis examples — HYPOTHETICAL, NOT DATA

**HYPOTHETICAL response:** A Host selects “Re-entering choices,” reports Q8=4, and chooses “Calculated manually” for H1.  
**Interpretation to test:** Rebuilding orders may create effort for some hosts.  
**Research follow-up:** Check interview timelines and whether the step occurs repeatedly.

**HYPOTHETICAL response:** A Member selects “Nothing” for M2, Q6=5, and “Open and explore” for Q11.  
**Interpretation to test:** Not all members face friction or distrust unfamiliar links.  
**Research follow-up:** Examine why that journey worked well.

**Do not write:** “Most Chennai customers struggle with splitting payments” unless an appropriate study supports that claim.
