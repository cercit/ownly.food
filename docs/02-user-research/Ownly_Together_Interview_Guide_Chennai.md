# Ownly Together — Chennai User Interview Guide

**Research status:** Discovery study; Ownly Together's Chennai launch and features are proposed, not confirmed live.  
**Product:** Group food orders: a host shares a link; members choose food; each person pays their share.  
**Primary research goal:** Understand group-ordering behaviour and obstacles to adoption.  
**Secondary goal:** Understand what drives repeat group orders.  
**North-star product metric:** Group orders completed per week (future measurement, not a research finding).  
**Personas:** Host (organiser/payer) and Member (participant).  
**Market:** Chennai — OMR, Velachery, T. Nagar, Adyar, Anna Nagar, Nungambakkam, Mylapore.

> **Research integrity:** All names, scenarios, and sample answers in this guide are **HYPOTHETICAL ILLUSTRATIONS**, not collected customer evidence. Interview participants about their *existing* experiences before describing Ownly Together. Do not claim Ownly Together is already available in Chennai.

## 1. Research questions and decisions

**Questions to answer**
- What causes a group to order together, and who does the coordination?
- Where do selection, ordering, payment, and follow-up become difficult—if at all?
- How do hosts and members decide which app or restaurant to use?
- What makes people join an unfamiliar service or repeat a shared order?

**Decisions informed**
- Whether to prioritize link joining, shared cart visibility, split payments, or reordering.
- Which Chennai audiences and occasions offer the best initial adoption path.
- What messages need proof before use in a launch campaign.

**Not a goal:** Proving that users like Ownly Together or estimating market-wide demand from a few interviews.

## 2. Recruiting and sampling

**Initial target:** 12 interviews: 6 Hosts, 6 Members; 25–35 minutes each. Cover office lunches, shared flats/PGs, and hostel/friend orders. Aim for variation across neighbourhoods, ordering frequency, and operating systems; do not imply a statistically representative sample.

**Screening criteria**
- Adult (18+) living, studying, or working in Chennai.
- Participated in a group food order within the last 3 months.
- Host: usually starts the order or handles checkout/payment.
- Member: usually joins rather than managing checkout.

**Recruitment channels:** Workplace teams, coworking communities, hostel/PG groups, neighbourhood WhatsApp communities (with admin permission).

**Avoid:** Recruiting only enthusiastic food-app users; placing supervisors and direct reports in the same interview; interviewing host and members together if seeking candid payment feedback.

## 3. Moderator setup and consent script

**Before interview:** Explain purpose, expected duration, voluntary participation, and whether notes/audio will be taken. State the *actual* data storage, access, retention period, and any incentive before starting. Avoid requesting UPI IDs, payment screenshots, order IDs, employer names, or other identifiable details.

**Say:**

> We are researching how people in Chennai arrange food orders with other people. This is not a sales call, and there are no right answers. We will discuss your real experiences, including what went smoothly. You may skip any question or stop at any time. May I take notes? Separately, may I record audio for research analysis? Recording is optional; declining will not affect participation.

**Record separately:** `Interview consent: Yes/No`; `Notes consent: Yes/No`; `Audio consent: Yes/No`. **If interview consent is no, stop.** Never record without explicit audio consent.

## 4. Interview flow (25–35 minutes)

| Part | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Consent and context | 3 min | Explain rights and establish role |
| Warm-up | 4 min | Understand habits and recent group order |
| Core JTBD and pain discovery | 15–20 min | Reconstruct actual decisions and events |
| Barriers and repeat behaviour | 4–5 min | Explore joining, trust, and habits |
| Wrap-up | 2–3 min | Surface unmet needs, invite correction |

**Ground rule:** Ask for the **last actual group order**, not what respondents imagine they might do. Follow neutral probes such as “What happened next?”, “Who handled that?”, “Can you recall an example?”, “How did you decide?”, and “Was there anything else?” Avoid praise or correcting responses.

## 5. Core questions 1–5: behaviour and coordination

Ask all five to both personas. Substitute “your group” for “you” if needed.

| # | Neutral question | Optional follow-up probe |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tell me about the most recent time you ordered food with other people. | Where were you; who was involved? |
| 2 | What triggered that order, and how did the group decide to order? | What other options did you consider? |
| 3 | Walk me through what happened from the first message to delivery. | Which apps, calls, or other steps were involved? |
| 4 | How were everyone's food choices collected and confirmed? | Who made changes, and when? |
| 5 | Which parts worked well, and which parts, if any, took extra effort? | What did that effort cost in time or money? |

**Do not ask:** “Was coordinating on WhatsApp annoying?” That assumes a channel and negative emotion.

## 6. Core questions 6–10: money, decisions, adoption

| # | Neutral question | Optional follow-up probe |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | How was the bill paid, and what happened afterward? | Who paid first; how were shares worked out? |
| 7 | How did you decide which restaurant or ordering service to use? | What mattered most in that decision? |
| 8 | What has made you order with the same group again—or not? | What was different from last time? |
| 9 | Think of the last time someone sent you a link to an unfamiliar app. What did you do? | What information influenced that choice? |
| 10 | If you could change one part of the last group order, what would it be? | Why that part rather than another? |

**Probe for real costs without assuming loss:** “Did anyone owe money afterward?” “How, if at all, was it resolved?” For Q9, let respondents discuss non-food apps if that is their actual example.

## 7. Persona-specific follow-up probes

**Hosts (ask selectively, not as a checklist)**
- How did you confirm the final cart before checkout?
- How did you track who had paid, if anyone owed money?
- What happened when someone's choice changed after the order began?
- What conditions would make you volunteer to coordinate again?

**Members**
- How did you communicate your choice and any customisation?
- When did you learn your share of the total?
- What would have made you opt out of that particular order?
- What helped you feel comfortable paying or joining?

**Language:** Offer English or Tamil as preferred. Keep wording plain and translate consistently; do not paraphrase away participants' original expressions in research notes.

## 8. Optional concept reaction (only after behavioural questions)

**Neutral concept card (read verbatim):**

> Imagine an optional food-ordering flow where someone starts a shared order, others join through a link, add food, and each pays their own share. It also keeps a group available for a future order.

Ask: “What stands out?”; “What, if anything, seems useful?”; “What questions or concerns would you have?”; “What, if anything, is missing?”; “In what situations would this not fit?”

**Avoid:** “Would you love using it?” and “How much time would this save?” Do **not** treat positive hypothetical reactions as evidence of actual adoption or ability to deliver the feature.

## 9. Wrap-up and note-taking template

**Wrap-up script:** “What have I not asked about group ordering that matters to you?” “Is there anything I misunderstood?” Thank them, reiterate any agreed next contact, and explain how they can withdraw data *if that option is actually supported by the study protocol*.

## 10. Illustrative responses — HYPOTHETICAL, NOT DATA

**HYPOTHETICAL host response:** “I sent a WhatsApp message at 12:15. Two people changed orders after I made the cart, so I called them before paying.”  
**Possible code:** `coordination / late changes / host / extra verification`.  
**Follow-up:** “What happened after those calls?”

**HYPOTHETICAL member response:** “I usually pay the organiser after lunch. I didn't know the delivery fee until they messaged the total.”  
**Possible code:** `payment visibility / late cost disclosure / member`.  
**Follow-up:** “How did you decide the amount was correct?”

**Interpretation caution:** Neither example shows that every group has the same problem; document smooth and failed experiences equally.

## 11. Bias audit and question revisions

| Avoid (bias type) | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| “Why is WhatsApp so confusing?” (leading) | “How were food choices communicated?” |
| “How much money did you lose?” (assumes loss) | “What happened after the bill was paid?” |
| “Would automatic split payment fix this?” (solution priming) | “How were payment shares managed?” |
| “Doesn't reordering save time?” (suggestive) | “What happened the next time you ordered together?” |
| “Why won't people install Ownly?” (brand and negative presupposition) | “Tell me about joining an unfamiliar app.” |
| “Which feature do you want most?” (premature feature framing) | “What would you change about your last experience?” |

**Quality checks:** One topic per question; ask about recent behaviour; use neutral language; separate actual experiences, observations, interpretations, and concept reactions.

## 12. Synthesis and analysis playbook

1. **Tag** each interview by role, use case, location, recent-order frequency, and consent-safe participant ID.
2. **Build an event timeline:** trigger → coordinate → choose → checkout → split/settle → delivery → repeat.
3. **Code evidence:** `JTBD`, `pain`, `gain`, `barrier`, `workaround`, `trigger`, `trust`, `repeat`, `positive`, `counterexample`.
4. **Cluster independently:** count *participants mentioning* each theme, not number of quotes. Segment hosts versus members.
5. **Rate opportunity:** user impact (1–3), evidence strength (1–3), reach across scenarios (1–3); label ratings as researcher judgments.
6. **Write findings** with a verbatim quote, observed example, denominator (e.g. 4 of 6 hosts), exception, and product implication.
7. **Prioritize experiments:** invite-link comprehension, clear per-person total, checkout trust, repeat-group re-entry.

**Do not infer prevalence from 12 interviews.** Validate promising patterns with a broader survey and real product tests when available.

## 13. Product hypotheses and measurement (not findings)

| Hypothesis to test | Future product metric | Research evidence to seek |
|---|---|---|
| Invite links reduce entry friction | Invite → join conversion | Prior unfamiliar-link decisions |
| Cart visibility reduces confusion | Join → cart-add conversion | Miscommunication events |
| Split checkout reduces host burden | Group orders completed/week | Settlement steps, unpaid-share incidents |
| Saved groups support repeat use | 28-day repeat-group rate | Actual repeat-order triggers |

Define a **completed group order** before instrumenting: e.g., one group checkout successfully placed, not merely a shared link created. This proposed definition requires product-team confirmation.

## 14. Fieldwork ethics and handoff checklist

- [ ] Eligibility screened; host/member mix monitored.
- [ ] Consent recorded; audio captured only if separately approved.
- [ ] No raw payment credentials, UPI IDs, phone numbers, or identifying order screenshots copied into notes.
- [ ] Real experiences distinguished from concept opinions.
- [ ] Quotes verified, de-identified, and available in the participant's original wording.
- [ ] Contradictory evidence preserved; no feature pitch during discovery.
- [ ] Findings labelled qualitative; examples labelled hypothetical.
- [ ] Research storage, deletion/withdrawal policy and retention period set before fieldwork.
